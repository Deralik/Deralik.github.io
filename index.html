From cluster to desktop — tera-scale volume data, interactive on one desktop GPU.

The method
the figure scrolls sideways ⟶
1the volume was compressed in situ, where it was computed — only the model travels to the desktop
2the renderer asks the cache manager for every sample, at the level of detail the view needs
3a missing sample falls back to whatever coarser brick covers it — only a region with no brick at any level is a true miss
4bricks the cache can't fill are batched to the async side; the renderer draws on and never waits
5the request handler ranks the queue by importance and has the INR generate the most salient bricks first
6new bricks land in the cache and its page tables update — indexed by a multi-resolution page directory sized for peta-scale data
7a true miss: the renderer falls back to the INR itself — rare once the coarsest level is preloaded

cache architecture based on the multi-resolution GPU cache of Sarton et al. for volume rendering

The abstract

Machine learning has enabled the use of implicit neural representations (INRs) to efficiently compress and reconstruct massive scientific datasets. However, despite advances in fast INR rendering algorithms, INR-based rendering remains computationally expensive, as computing data values from an INR is significantly slower than reading them from GPU memory. This bottleneck currently restricts interactive INR visualization to professional workstations. To address this challenge, we introduce an INR rendering framework accelerated by a scalable, multi-resolution GPU cache capable of efficiently representing tera-scale datasets. By minimizing redundant data queries and prioritizing novel volume regions, our method reduces the number of INR computations per frame, achieving an average 5x speedup over the state-of-the-art INR rendering method while still maintaining high visualization quality. Coupled with existing hardware-accelerated INR compressors, our framework enables scientists to generate and compress massive datasets in situ on high-performance computing platforms and then interactively explore them on consumer-grade hardware post hoc.

First author’s note

The cache builds on Sarton et al.’s multi-resolution design; my work was adapting it to read from an INR instead of the data directly. However, testing revealed an opportunity for further optimization: the original loaded data on a first-come, first-served basis. I eventually landed on a simple ranking operation to keep track of the most sampled bricks, which further increased performance.

GRTCache

A radiance cache you can look at — the volume's light field held in world-space gaussians, trained while it renders.

The method
The pipeline — abstract
1a PCD pass casts first rays and keeps the hit positions — the gaussians seed where the medium is (once, dashed)
2the loop streams training samples: bounce positions + directions, NEE at each bounce, and the composition trace-back
3the optimization step fits the gaussians; MCMC relocates unused ones, adds new ones, and nudges the rest
4in use, rays terminate into the cache mid-path — the bounces they skip are the savings
The training step — traced back · after NRC (Müller et al. 2021)
1a rendering path terminates into the cache — the same termination heuristic as NRC
2a portion of rays extend past the termination — training suffixes, bounced further only to learn
3walking back along the ray, radiance composes into a training target at every vertex; NEE at each bounce adds direct-light samples

training scheme and termination heuristic follow NRC — Müller et al. 2021; what differs is what holds the radiance
